Field Shop Stats

Everything on this page comes straight from the installed kaggriculture.py engine's own constants (SHOPS, MARKET_PARAMS, market_price(), _town_consume()) — not from any played match or downloaded replay. It's about the shop mechanic itself: what each shop actually consumes, and what that implies for town-wide demand and market price if the 8 reveals land the way the engine draws them (uniformly, with replacement — matching what the Worlds/Shops-by-day pages already observe empirically). The "Worlds" and per-day-reveal statistics you played or downloaded live on the Global/Worlds tab; this page never touches that dataset.

Shop draw probability

Every reveal (day 3, 6, 9, ... 24) draws one of the 8 shop types with replacement. The engine draws uniformly — the Worlds page's own per-day pies confirm this empirically across every real replay. Everything below assumes this same 1-in-8 baseline for whichever reveals haven't happened yet.

Production capacity vs. consumption pressure

How fast can a farm actually replace what the town is draining? Steady-state per-tile (or per-animal) output is read straight from the same crop/animal constants this project's own valuation.py uses for its future-harvest projections: a one-shot crop (WHEAT/CARROT/MELON) at max_yield / max_yield_day, assuming an immediate replant each cycle; an "ongoing" crop (TOMATO/STRAWBERRY) or an animal at 1 / interval once it's mature. This deliberately ignores the market (no price-driven behavior change) and the opponent (no competition for tiles) -- it's a raw capacity number, not a strategy.

"Tiles/animals to break even" = expected daily consumption (from the demand table below) divided by this steady-state rate, rounded up -- roughly how many of that crop/animal a farm would need dedicated to it just to keep pace with the town's own draw, before selling a single unit for profit.

What each shop actually consumes

Every townShopSellInterval steps (4 by default — 6 times a day), each currently-unlocked shop instance pulls its own products out of the market's inventory. A shop is drawn with replacement once every 3 days (8 draws total, by day 24) and, once unlocked, never stops consuming — so by late game several instances of the same shop can be active at once. A shop with only one product consumes it at double the rate of a shop with several (the engine's own multiplier = 2 if len(products) == 1 else 1).

Expected town demand per product, assuming random draws

Model: each of the 8 reveal slots independently draws one of the 8 shop types with equal probability, with replacement (so a shop type can repeat, and its expected instance count after N reveals is N/8). "Town center" is a separate, shop-independent baseline: 1 unit of every product except FERTILIZER, once a day, from step 0 regardless of what's unlocked.

Projected market price, consumption only

Starting every product at the engine's own I0 (10,000, the price-neutral inventory level) and applying only the expected demand above — no player buying or selling at all — this is market_price() run forward against that one-sided drain, day by day through the 8 reveals and on to day 30, the engine's actual default game length (episodeSteps=720) — 6 more days of every shop still consuming after the 8th and last one opens. It isolates what the shop mechanic alone does to price; real games also have players selling into the market (which raises inventory back up) and buying out of it (which lowers it further), so actual prices move differently. This is the shape of the pressure shops apply, not a prediction of any real match's prices.

Price range: worst / expected / best, GIVEN the last K reveals are guaranteed to fit

Same consumption-only projection to day 30 (the engine's own default episode length, episodeSteps=720 -- 6 full days past the 8th and final reveal, still consuming the whole time). The premise for each K column is fixed: the LAST K reveals (K=3, 4, 5, 6) are guaranteed to land on whichever single shop drains this product fastest, in every one of the 3 numbers -- that part never varies, since it's the whole point of "the last K are ideal." What actually varies is only the remaining (8−K) EARLY reveals, whose outcome genuinely isn't known yet: "worst" assumes they also miss this product entirely (nothing extra beyond the guaranteed late run); "expected" assumes their real average value; "best" assumes they ALSO happen to land on that same best shop. So "worst" is never lower than what the guaranteed late shops alone would produce -- it's a floor built ON TOP of the guarantee, not a scenario where the guarantee might not have happened. A product touched by many shops already (WHEAT: 5 of 8) has a high floor even at "worst," while its own worst-to-best RANGE stays narrow if its price-response function is a damped one (log/sqrt) rather than a sharp one (hinge/sq) -- being consumed everywhere and being volatile are two different things.

A product no shop ever touches (MELON, FERTILIZER) has no range at all -- worst, expected, and best are all identical, since luck in the shop draw can't do anything for it.

Price over the full 30-day game

Pick one product -- the chart assumes the last K reveals are guaranteed to land on THAT product's own best shop. Almost every shop serves several products at once (YARN_STORE and PET_CAFE are the only single-product exceptions), so that same guaranteed shop is also quietly driving up demand for whatever else it consumes -- tick those "also affected" products below to put them on the same axes. Toggle worst/expected/best independently to cut clutter, and optionally lay a sale on top: a single one-time dump, or repeated selling timed to that product's own harvest cycle from the Capacity table above (a melon farm can't turn over stock every day the way a carrot farm can, so the repeat interval is read from the crop/animal itself, never a free-floating slider).
