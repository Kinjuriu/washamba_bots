### A new test built on the opponents that actually decide our rating (MEASURED)

The Bradley-Terry point you raised is the key one. The final score only counts win, loss or tie, and matchmaking pairs us with agents rated near us. Among the opponents our submissions met between 2,000 and 2,800, about 96% are tape-family agents; the reactive agents only start around 2,800. So the number to improve is **our win rate against the tape band**, not bank size and not the top six.

I built a "band panel": 233 real ladder games our submissions (W0, W1, W3) played against tape-family opponents rated 2,200 to 2,800. Each opponent is replayed from its recording on the original seed and seat, and a candidate plays our side. W3 reproduces its own 61 recorded games exactly, so the test is faithful. 222 games stay faithful under both W3 and each candidate.

## My view on Fable's review

Where I agree: constant searches are finished; W3 does not have an inventory or liquidation problem (it converts stock to cash faster than the top six); the slot churn of 21 to 25 September cost us more than any agent change; 3,000 is not reachable with a W3-based agent.

Where I differ:
- Fable's harvest front-running (W5) looked like a real edge on its seeds. It did not replicate on mine, and it costs money against reactive agents. I would not upload it.
- Fable framed the rest of the week as "hold W3". The band panel says W6 is a measured, low-risk improvement over W3 where our rating is decided, so holding W3 alone leaves a small gain on the table.
- Fable says W3's final rating should land around 2,600 to 2,750. That is inference; my fit of W3's 84 ladder games puts its strength near 2,490 on the ladder scale. Expect the high 2,400s to low 2,600s, with W6 perhaps 40 to 70 points above W3.

On the Bradley-Terry model: I fitted W3's 84 games with one rating and compared actual to expected wins by opponent family and band. The residuals are within noise (tape family +0.4 wins, 2,500 to 2,800 band -1.3 wins), so there is no hidden matchup where W3 underperforms its rating in our band. Non-transitive effects exist above 2,800, where we rarely play. Near a 50% win rate, each extra percentage point of wins is worth about 7 rating points.

On reverse-engineering the top six (Fable's POLICY_INFERENCE.md): I agree with the smallest mechanism Fable found: a scripted opening to about step 150, land bought on a clock, a herd sized by a table keyed to the unlocked shops, and premium goods sold in small lots each time the shops drain. It is copyable as rules, but the hard part is the day-to-day labour scheduler, and the splice attempt lost 0-32 on exactly that. Not a three-day job.
