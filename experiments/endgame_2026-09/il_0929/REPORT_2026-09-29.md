# Imitation learning and policy improvement for the final agent: assessment and tests
Tuesday 29 September 2026, Fable 5.1, local Mac (Apple M3, 8 cores, kaggle-environments 1.32.7). MEASURED means run here with the file and sample size named; INFERENCE means judgement. Everything new is in `il_0929/`. No existing agent was changed, nothing was pushed or submitted.

## Summary
The DAgger paper explains the ranker failure exactly, and its remedy is not available to us: the experts are recordings that cannot be asked what to do in the states our own controller reaches. The only expert we can query is the engine itself, so I used it the way the post-2011 literature does (AggreVaTe and Expert Iteration: label a decision by rolling the base policy forward, distil the answer into a rule) on the one decision the traces flagged as wasteful, Step1009's late sheep purchases in worlds with no yarn store. The rule lost every decided game (drop variant 0-52-12 over 64 paired games on two seed sets, mean about -7,100; cow variant 0-28-4 over 32, mean -9,556), for a reason the traces make clear: the shop sequence is unknown when the sheep are bought, and animals earn fertilizer every day regardless of their product's market, so Step1009's herd rule is already close to optimal under uncertainty. A second attempt, grafting W6's harvest-triggered front-runner (the one learned-from-data layer that ever measured positive) onto Step1009, is vacuous: Step1009 sells on arrival and never holds stock to sell early. Nothing from this session merits a submission slot; keep Step1009 as the final lineage.

## 1. What Ross, Gordon and Bagnell (2011) say about our results
INFERENCE from the paper, applied to MEASURED team results in `tonight_0928/RESULTS_2026-09-28.md`.
- Behaviour cloning trained on the expert's states with per-step error ε can make on the order of T²ε mistakes over T steps under the distribution of states the learner itself induces (their Theorem 2.1, from Ross and Bagnell 2010). One mistake leads to states the expert never demonstrated, where the classifier is unsupported, and the errors compound. The ranker is that case: 37.6% held-out agreement against 25.1% for Peter's weights, yet 0-16 in full games with wheat falling to 7 tiles and 13 weeds on seed 903; T is 720 here.
- DAgger's fix is to run the learner, ask the expert for the right action in the learner's own states, aggregate and retrain; the guarantee drops to order Tε plus a no-regret term, but only if the expert can be queried at arbitrary states. Our experts are 160 recorded top-8 wins; when our agent plays differently the recordings stop being faithful (48 of 160 games for Step1009). So DAgger proper cannot be run, and more data or a better network does not remove the T² term.
- What came after 2011 and whether it applies here. AggreVaTe (Ross and Bagnell 2014) labels states with cost-to-go; Expert Iteration and AlphaZero-style methods (Anthony, Tian and Barber 2017; Silver et al. 2017; Guo et al. 2014) replace the human expert with a planner in the simulator. We have the exact engine, so this is the one legitimate route; at 5.1 s per game and about 500 paired games an hour on this Mac it supports labelling a handful of discrete decisions, not a 720-step labour policy. DART (Laskey et al. 2017) and adversarial imitation (GAIL and successors) need the expert in the loop or an RL loop over millions of steps. Residual policy learning (Silver et al. 2018; Johannink et al. 2019) is the shape every layer that ever helped this team has had: a small correction on a strong base whose state distribution stays in the regime the base was tuned for.
- Why the evidence points away from neural methods for this deadline: there is no queryable expert; the reward that decides games is a few hundred coins of near-tie margin on a 100k bank, so a learned controller must match a hand-tuned tape's execution before any gain shows; and the 18k gap to the top-8 is production planning under shop uncertainty with a reactive scheduler, which 160 replays with 4% coverage of BUILD actions and no pickup, drop or place cannot teach.

## 2. Hypothesis 1: demand-keyed late herd (MEASURED, ruled out)
Evidence that motivated it (traces `il_0929/trace_mirror_0.json`, `_1.json`): Step1009 buys 2 sheep at step 1 and 4 more at steps 196 to 226 in every world; with no yarn store the wool price hit the $1 floor at step 344 and stayed there for 321 of the remaining 376 steps; 64 of 130 wool units were ordered at the floor. The wool market above I0 absorbs about 60 units before the floor (price 200 minus 0.058 x squared), and the town center drains one unit a day.

Build (`il_0929/make_variant.py`): the exact Step1009 file plus an appended layer that touches only the late BUY_ANIMAL SHEEP orders, and only while no yarn store is unlocked: `hA_drop.py` removes them, `hA_cow.py` converts them to cows (same pasture structure). Labels came from paired full games, the apprentice is a shop-keyed rule; that is the AggreVaTe shape.

| test (`il_0929/run_match.py`, both seats, one subprocess per game) | result |
|---|---|
| hA_drop vs Step1009, seeds 900-915 (`m_hA_drop_vs_base.txt`) | 0-28-4, mean margin -7,077, n=32 |
| hA_cow vs Step1009, seeds 900-915 (`m_hA_cow_vs_base.txt`) | 0-28-4, mean -9,556, n=32 |

| yarn store unlocks after day 8 (8 seeds) | mean -12,520, range -5,020 to -21,764 |
| yarn store open by day 6, overlay never fires (2 seeds) | exact ties |

Why it loses. (a) At the purchase step only two or three shops are known; shops are drawn with replacement every three days, so a yarn store arrives later in about half the worlds (8 of 16 seeds here), and then the four sheep are worth 5k to 22k of wool. (b) Even where no yarn store ever comes, the sheep pay: every animal offers fertilizer daily, and in the exactly re-simulated ladder tie game 114809656 Step1009 sold 327 fertilizer for 14,921, 11.7% of its sale revenue (`resim_ladder_losses.jsonl`). The money-gap path in the never-yarn seeds shows the variant ahead by 1,846 at step 240 and behind from step 450 on, with identical hires and seed purchases, so the reversal is the missing animals' output. The wool at $1 is the price of an option that pays in most worlds; Step1009's herd rule is close to optimal under shop uncertainty, and this is the strongest hypothesis ruled out today.

## 3. Hypothesis 2: harvest-triggered front-running on Step1009 (MEASURED, vacuous)
Rationale: predicting a stationary tape opponent from its public state is the one supervised-learning use that DAgger's covariate shift does not touch, and W6's dump tables learned from recordings are the only such component that measured positive (band share 0.667 vs 0.601 for W3). Build `il_0929/hB_fr.py`: Step1009 plus the 635-line W6 overlay (it depends only on FarmView and projected_shed, both defined in Step1009) plus a latch that turns it off for the rest of the game when the rival's money equals ours on every step through step 150.

Results: against Step1009 on seed 900, both seats, exact ties (`m_hB_sanity.jsonl`), so the latch holds. Against v57 on seed 7100 the predictor identified the W0 family and predicted 15 dumps (103 units) within six steps, but Step1009 held only 45 units in total at those moments and the layer added 0 orders; against k0013 and fieldcraft no family was detected and 0 orders were added (`telemetry_two.py`). Step1009 sells on arrival; its shed is empty at almost every step in the traces, so there is nothing to sell ahead of a dump. The candidate's actions are Step1009's, so the recorded-opponent panels were not run for it. For reference, the team's panel files check out: Step1009 is faithful in 48 of 48 ladder replays (`tonight_0928/lad.jsonl`) and 222 of 233 band replays (`band_new.jsonl`).

## 4. Levers inspected and rejected without a full test
- Mirrors. Step1009 self-play, seeds 900-915, both seats: 2-2-28, 28 exact ties (`m_selfplay.txt`); the four decided games are the seeds where the shared weed RNG treats the two farms differently. In the engine both players' same-index SELL units are quoted at the same pre-commit inventory, so lockstep selling is jointly more profitable than selling in sequence; whoever deviates goes second on some item and loses about b·n(n+1)/2 while gaining at most b·m(m-1)/2 on another, which is the pattern of the team's five final-day variants (1-15 each). The v15stack lineage already carries "exact best-response ordering against a detected clone" (W6 file, lines 6035 and 6826). INFERENCE: no cheap mirror-breaker exists.
- Last-day waste. On day 29 Step1009 issues no FEED, PLANT, FERTILIZE or PICKUP; watering inside a crop's window still adds yield the same day (`lastdays.py`, seed 0). Nothing to save.
- Production mix. The 75 owned tiles are full from day 10 to day 22 (17 animals, 32 strawberries, 24-25 wheat; `tiles.py`); strawberries are 40% of revenue at an average 207 against a base of 120, but the counts come from routes, not constants, and the fourth quadrant is never bought. Expanding is the scheduler problem already on the ruled-out list.
- Population (48 ladder games): 8 opponents tracked Step1009's money exactly to step 146 or later; they are other versions of the same public notebook, differing in small market details (an extra wheat sale at step 245, a wool unit held one step, an empty trailing order). Against them: 2 wins (+428, +542), 2 exact ties, 4 losses (-36 to -355). The large losses (-2.8k to -6k) were to tape forks with other openings (12 melon seeds at turn 1; land at 149 and 224). 7 of 12 losses were under 500.
